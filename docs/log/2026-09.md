### The Cardputer was full — six of six capped lanes at cap, and two different reasons

*2026-09-30*

Phase A owed one board a filesystem flash: the RFC corpus went 38 → 44 records when the
five `TTG-RFC-*` were imported, and the T-Deck had it (`RFC globe loaded: 52692 bytes, 44
records`) while the Cardputer still carried the old corpus. `mklittlefs` images the whole
`data/` directory and the partition is written in one shot, so a filesystem flash **resets
the runtime TTDB to its seed** — and the Cardputer is the only board in the fleet that can
author a belief (`PerceptLearn` Rule 1 arms only off a `still` `@LAT95` window, and only it
has an IMU). So: pull first. The newest backup was `master/cardputer_1.md` from
**2026-07-31** at 63,502 B — two months stale, the same gap that nearly cost the T-Deck's
data earlier the same day.

The pull came back at **137,958 bytes, 257 records** — more than double the July backup,
and 2.5× the T-Deck's. Banked at `master/cardputer_pre_actIII_2026-09-30.md`, verified
byte-exact against the 3,629 B seed for its first 3,629 bytes with 134 KB of runtime
records appended beneath, 0 CR, 0 NUL, valid UTF-8, clean final record.

**Every capped lane the board writes was exactly at its cap:**

| lane | records | cap constant | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `@LAT90` timeline | 16 | `TIMESTREAM_MAX_LANE 16` | `TimeStreamNode.h:39` |
| `@LAT92` outcomes | 24 | `PERCEPTLEARN_MAX_LANE 24` | `PerceptLearn.h:156` |
| `@LAT94` acoustic | 48 | `ACOUSTICPERCEPT_MAX_LANE 48` | `AcousticPercept.h:37` |
| `@LAT95` motion | 48 | `MOTIONPERCEPT_MAX_LANE 48` | `MotionPercept.h:125` |
| `@LAT96` entity | 48 | `ENTITYPERCEPT_MAX_LANE 48` | `EntityPercept.h:36` |
| `@LAT97` link | 48 | `LINKPERCEPT_MAX_LANE 48` | `LinkPercept.h:33` |
| `@LAT100` lanegen | 6 | `LANEGEN_MAX_LANE 32` | room |
| `@LAT101` field | 5 | **none, by design** | room |
| `@LAT91` beliefs | 8 | — | |
| `@LAT93` transitions | 2 | — | |

Six of six. **The only lanes with room were the two that have no cap** — and `@LAT101` is
the fleet's first FIELD lane (TTDB-RFC-0010 §5 stage 3), whose whole point is
decay-on-read with no prune path. The lane built to test "never refuses a write" was the
one still accepting writes. That is not a coincidence; it is the stage-3 falsifier
reporting a pass nobody asked it for.

Whole-file index: **257 of `TTDB_MAX_RECORDS` 288** — 89 per cent, 31 slots from the
ceiling where [[ttdb-index-shared-budget]] records that overflowing the index let a lane
prune *silently delete every record past the cap*. The board was one busy hour from the
defect zone.

⚠ **The six saturated lanes were failing in TWO DIFFERENT WAYS, and the distinction
matters more than the count.** `@LAT100` holds six `**LANE-PRUNED**` markers:

```
**LANE-PRUNED** lane:96 gen:1 removed:48 last_lon:47 stream:0x00000000
**LANE-PRUNED** lane:97 gen:1 removed:48 last_lon:47 stream:0x00000000
**LANE-PRUNED** lane:94 gen:1 removed:48 last_lon:47 stream:0x00000000
**LANE-PRUNED** lane:94 gen:2 removed:48 last_lon:47 stream:0x1a8126a3
**LANE-PRUNED** lane:95 gen:1 removed:48 last_lon:47 stream:0x7eeae016
**LANE-PRUNED** lane:97 gen:2 removed:48 last_lon:47 stream:0x00000000
```

Every one is `removed:48` — a **whole-lane wipe**, not an eviction of the oldest few. So:

- **`@LAT94`–`@LAT97` were TREADMILLING.** Fill 48, delete all 48, refill. Six boundaries
  × 48 = **288 percept windows destroyed**, and at the moment of the pull all four lanes
  were full again, queued for wipes seven through ten. The lane never refused a write; it
  paid for each one by discarding forty-eight.
- **`@LAT90` and `@LAT92` were REFUSING.** Neither has an automatic prune path —
  `lanegen::pruneTimeline` and `lanegen::pruneOutcomes` are separately named,
  operator-invoked calls — so neither lane has a marker, and both sit *exactly* at cap.
  New records were being dropped.

The second mode is the serious one, because **`Reconciler` is a pure function of
`@LAT92`**. With that lane stuck at 24/24, the eight `@LAT91` beliefs were being
recomputed every Dream Cycle from a tally that had stopped accepting evidence — frozen at
`TALLY met:11 violated:0 unobserved:0`. A belief that cannot be moved by new experience is
not a belief any more; it is a constant with provenance attached.

📎 Four of the six prune markers carry `stream:0x00000000`, which is *meaningful* rather
than broken — local `millis()`, comparable with nothing but that node's own records. Those
prunes ran before the node had a stream, i.e. inside `setup()`, which is consistent with
[[lane-generations]]: a prune refused for heap is scheduled in NVS and executed at the next
boot, where ~102 KB is available instead of ~7 KB.

**On what this does and does not establish.** The census and the six markers are
observations. The treadmill reading is direct — the markers state it. The *refusal* reading
for `@LAT90`/`@LAT92` is an inference from three facts (both at exactly their cap; neither
has a prune marker; both lanes' documented policy is refuse-on-full), and I did not watch a
write be refused. It is strong, and it is still inference from a single census. The cheap
confirmation is to watch one lane across a boot with the node untouched; that is worth
doing inside Phase C rather than before it, because C2's mechanisms replace the policy
either way.

What this changes for Phase C: nothing in the plan, everything in its standing. §5 argued
from the *shape* of the lane disciplines that a capped, prune-per-lane memory cannot be the
memory of an agent. This is that argument's measurement — unprompted, on hardware, on the
one board that can form a belief. **ACT-III §C2's three non-filling mechanisms now have a
before-picture.**

#### A dedup that looked wrong and was not

The catchboot trace showed:

```
[stream] adopted adds nothing: @LAT90 already explains stream 0x4a194eda — no record written
```

on a filesystem flashed seconds earlier, whose seed contains four records, no `@LAT90`, and
no mention of that stream id. That reads as a false dedup hit on an empty lane — the
needle-collision family this project has paid for three times.

It is not. `Upload-Cardputer-FS.ps1` ends with `esptool`'s `Hard resetting via RTS pin`, so
the board had **already booted once** before catchboot reset it a second time, and wrote
`@LAT90LON0` on that first boot. Confirmed by re-pulling: exactly one `@LAT90` record,
`**STREAM-ADOPTED** stream:0x4a194eda wall:0 node:0x300 from:0x100`. The second boot
correctly wrote nothing — "a reboot that rejoins the stream the node was already on writes
nothing", working as designed.

📎 Worth noting `from:0x100`: the Cardputer adopted its timeline **from the K10**, whose
stream clock read 7.4 h. Older stream wins, and the fleet agreed about time with no laptop
involved in the decision.

⚠ **What the trace did surface is a real documentation defect.** The emitted verbs are
`**STREAM-ORIGIN**`, `**STREAM-ADOPTED**`, `**STREAM-RECONCILED**`, `**STREAM-ANCHORED**`
(`buildStreamRecord`, `TimeStream.cpp:348–351`) — all four `STREAM-`prefixed. CLAUDE.md
documented them as `STREAM-ORIGIN`/`ADOPTED`/`RECONCILED`/`ANCHORED`, so **three of the
four names would not have matched the string they name.** Anyone writing a reader from the
docs would have got a needle that silently found nothing — exactly the failure that cost
this project the `prev_stream:` trap, the leading-space `" stream:0x"` needle, and
`**COVERED-SPAN**` in `@LAT92`. Fixed in CLAUDE.md, with the miss recorded so the class
stays visible.

### Phase C, increment 1: the consolidator, and a third way the old store refused

*2026-09-30*

`firmware/libraries/Semantic/` implements TTG-RFC-0003 §2 as the fleet's default
consolidator, with `tests/test_semantic.cpp` holding **88 checks, 0 failures**. The suite is
now **34/34** (17 native + 16 laptop + the Makefile guard).

It holds both of ACT-III §C3's pre-registered gates:

- **GATE 1 — identical under reordered episodes.** Four different episode orders produce a
  byte-identical belief line. The property is structural rather than tested-into-existence:
  TTG-0003 §2 says `for = Σ plus` over episodes, and a sum is commutative.
- **GATE 2 — identical after evicting the oldest episodes with fold-before-forget.** Also
  byte-identical, including with *every* episode evicted, at which point the belief rests
  entirely on the carried tally.

📐 **The design decision that made GATE 2 easy was streaming.** Because A32-RFC-0002 forbids
loading the TTDB whole, the consolidator is a fold the caller drives
(`beginEpisode` / `percept` / `endEpisode`) rather than a pass over a loaded lane. That turns
fold-before-forget into a **move** — `endEpisode(EVICTING)` subtracts the dying episode's
per-episode max from `live_` and adds it to `carried_` — so `live_ + carried_` is invariant
*arithmetically*, not approximately and not merely for-this-consumer. TTG-0002 §5.1's rule
that an episode MUST NOT be modified after it is written is what makes the replayed max equal
the max originally added, which is what makes the move sound rather than plausible.

The carried tally is serialised as its own `carried:` line, never as a `belief:` line, and the
test pins three properties of the bytes: it contains no `percept:`, it does not begin with the
belief key, and neither key is a substring of the other. That is the `@LAT92` gravestone rule
generalised — a carried tally is data *about* evidence, never evidence — and the test also
feeds the gravestone back in to confirm it does not parse as a percept.

#### Both consolidators, over the fleet's own 24 outcome records

`scratchpad/consolidator_compare.py` runs Rule 3 and TTG-0003 counting over the banked
`@LAT92` lane. Run-length is expanded back to windows first, because a `**COVERED** …
windows:N` line is N observations and treating it as one discards N−1 of them *in the
flattering direction* (the windows run-length drops are the ones where nothing changed). The
check that the expansion is faithful is not an assertion: the summed windows must reproduce
the `**TALLY** met:/violated:` the node itself wrote. **They do, on 8 of 8 claims that have a
belief.**

```
peer         proto      met  vio    rule3   count  delta
0x00000010   ble         47    0      222     250    +28
0x00000010   espnow      47    0      222     250    +28
0x00000011   ble         40    4      144     227    +83
0x00000011   espnow      39    0      206     249    +43
0x00000012   ble         11    0      150     235    +85
0x00000012   espnow      11    0      150     235    +85
0x00000100   espnow      31    0      190       —    no belief   <-- see below
0x00000200   ble         47    2      190     240    +50
0x00000200   espnow      44    5      136     225    +89
```

Counting is higher on **8 of 8**, mean 177.5 → 238.9, largest gap **+89**.

⚠ **One claim I made before measuring was wrong, and the measurement is the reason to trust
the rest.** I expected the two consolidators to *invert a ranking*, because Rule 3's conf
tracks how MUCH was seen while counting's tracks how CONSISTENT it was. On this data the
ordering **agrees** — both rank `0x10/ble` first — and what differs is magnitude. That still
matters, because EPS is a magnitude and not a rank: `0x200/espnow` carries
EPS ∝ (255 − 136) = 119 under Rule 3 versus (255 − 225) = 30 under counting, a **4× difference
in how much attention that belief attracts**. The honest statement is that the two
consolidators agree about order here and disagree about confidence by up to 89 points, and
that the attention consequence follows from the magnitude, not from an inversion.

#### The third refusal mode, and it is the worst one

There are **nine** claims in the evidence and the node held **eight** beliefs. The missing one
is `0x00000100 espnow` — **the K10**, with **31 confirmations and zero violations**.

`PERCEPTLEARN_MAX_CLAIMS 8` sets `PERCEPTLEARN_MAX_BELIEFS`, and the Dream Cycle drops the
overflow. So alongside the two failure modes already recorded above there is a third, and it
is the least visible of the three:

| tier | mode | recorded where? |
|---|---|---|
| `@LAT94`–`@LAT97` | **treadmill** — whole-lane wipe, refill | ✅ a `**LANE-PRUNED**` boundary in `@LAT100` |
| `@LAT90`, `@LAT92` | **refuse** — lane at cap, no prune path | ❌ nothing |
| `@LAT91` beliefs | **dropped at the RAM table** | ❌ a serial line, gone at the next reboot |

The sketch already names the consequence precisely, which is why this is a design fault and
not a bug: *"A dropped claim biases conf from a subset of the lane while looking like a
complete fold."* The store shows eight beliefs and nothing whatsoever indicating a ninth was
formed and discarded. The fleet had thirty-one confirmations that its link to the K10 was
stable and **could not hold the conclusion** — and the only witness was a `printf` on a cable
that was not attached.

✅ **This is the case `Semantic`'s reclaim-lowest was written for, and it answers it on both
counts.** A full table drops the **lowest-EPS** term, not the newest claim — the store gives
up what it neither relies on (low sal) nor doubts (high conf), which is principled where
"whoever arrived ninth" is arbitrary. And every reclamation increments `reclaimed()`, so the
forgetting is **countable from outside the process** rather than announced to a serial port.
The test overflows the table twice over and asserts `refused() == 0`, which is ACT-III §C0's
pass condition checked rather than asserted.

build flags — the same family as the four undefined variables that Makefile is notorious for.
Fixed, along with adding `test_semantic`. And `check_makefile.py` earned its keep within

keeps its own source list. Two runners disagreeing is exactly what that guard is for.
