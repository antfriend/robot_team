### Phase C, increment 2: the EPISODE lane, and why the checkpoint is the commit point

*2026-10-01*

ACT-III §C2's second tier is built: `firmware/libraries/Semantic/src/Episode.{h,cpp}`,
portable and native-tested, plus one new `Ttdb` primitive. **Not yet wired into a sketch
and not yet compiled for the ESP32** — it is a library the next increment calls.

**The lanes.** `@LAT103` EPISODE (one `ttdb-episode` block per record, TTG-RFC-0002 §5.1)
and `@LAT104` CARRIED (the fold checkpoint). Below 90 is a node record to both globes;
`@LAT102` is reserved for attributed testimony. They sit beside `@LAT93`–`97` during the
transition, and C0's deletion removes those, not these.

**The design problem the increment was really about.** C2c's fold-before-forget was built
in increment 1 as a move in RAM (live → carried, total invariant). On flash it meets the
fact that a `Ttdb` has exactly one way to delete: a whole-file rewrite with seven named
failure modes, three of which are "out of heap with the radios up", measured on hardware.
A fold that is only safe if the delete succeeds would reproduce the deferred-prune
machinery C5 is meant to delete. So the order is:

1. **FOLD** in RAM (replay the oldest live episodes `EVICTING`).
2. **COMMIT** by *appending* one `@LAT104` checkpoint: `through: <ordinal>` + every term's
   carried tally. An append is the one write a `Ttdb` does not rewrite for.
3. **CUT** — `Ttdb::removeCuts` — episodes at or below the **committed** horizon, and every
   checkpoint but the newest.

Boot reads only the newest checkpoint and **skips** episodes at or below its `through:`
even if they are still on flash. A cut therefore stops being bookkeeping and becomes
garbage collection: a crash between any two steps, or a cut the heap refuses forever, costs
space and nothing else. That is the property the deferred-prune-on-next-boot NVS machinery
was approximating, obtained by ordering instead of by retry.

**Three decisions made in passing, each written into `Episode.h`:**

- **Oldest-first eviction, not lowest-EPS.** C2c says eviction must not be recency. For
  *episodes* the order cannot move a belief (TTG-0003 §2 is a sum and the fold is a move);
  it only chooses which provenance survives. Oldest-first keeps the retained window
  contiguous, so the horizon is **one integer** rather than a set on flash, and it never
  re-points a citation — ordinals are max+1, the oldest is never the max, so unlike a
  `@LAT94`–`97` prune (lane empties, restarts at LON0) no `@LAT100` boundary is needed.
  Lowest-EPS stays where it belongs: the TERM tier's reclaim.
- **Ordinals are serial numbers mod 32768.** `TtdbRecord::lon` is `int16_t`, and a lane
  that never restarts reaches 32767 in ~23 days at one episode a minute. Every older/newer
  is an age measured back from the newest record in **file order**. ⚠ This makes a raw
  ordinal citation ambiguous across a wrap, so **C4's `follows@` edges should carry a sid**
  (TTDB-RFC-0010 §4.3), not a bare ordinal.
- **Only the newest checkpoint is ever read.** A term the TERM tier reclaimed is absent
  from later checkpoints; reading an older one too would resurrect a belief the store
  chose to forget. `CheckpointReader` honours exactly one ordinal.

**The gravestone rule holds in three independent layers** (no checkpoint line begins
`percept:`/`belief:`; a `carried:` line has four pipe columns where a percept needs six;
and the reader feeds only `ttdb-episode` blocks on `@LAT103`). Each is tested on its own.

**Measured, `tests/test_episode.cpp` — 82 checks, 0 failures.** The protocol runs end to
end against a stand-in store, with a **reboot from the store alone after every episode**:

| scenario | episodes | commits (failed) | cuts (failed) | max on flash |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean, ring 8 / batch 3 | 300 | 73 (0) | 73 (0) | 8 |
| cuts refused half the time | 300 | 73 (0) | 145 (72) | 9 |
| commits fail 1 in 3 | 300 | 109 (36) | 73 (0) | 9 |
| both | 300 | 109 (36) | 145 (72) | 10 |
| across the 32768 wrap, both faults | 200 | 71 (23) | 95 (47) | 10 |
| fleet constants 48 / 8, cuts refused half | 400 | 40 (0) | 78 (39) | 49 |

In every scenario: every episode appended (none refused), the node's beliefs equal a
never-evicting reference at every step, and the reboot agrees at every step. Worst-case
checkpoint (32 terms, maximal lemmas and tallies) is **3,945 B of a 4,416 B buffer**,
pinned both ways (fits; one byte short writes nothing).

📐 **Negative controls, both red:** cutting at the RAM horizon instead of the committed
one is caught by the reboot gate (only in the commit-failure scenarios, which is exactly
where it is wrong); a boot that ignores the horizon fails 17 checks. `test_ttdb_index`
gains a `removeCuts` section (10 checks, through the real `Ttdb` via `tests/shim/`),
including that file order survives the rewrite, since the ring reads the newest episode
off it. Suite **35/35**.

📎 Housekeeping: `tests/Makefile`'s `clean` recipe had carried literal `\n` text instead of
line continuations since the `ttdb_index`/`semantic` targets were added; fixed in passing.

**What is next, in order:** the sketch glue (`EpisodeNode.h`, the `LaneGenNode` shape —
boot scan, append, fold/commit/cut from `loop()`), one sampler emitting `percept:` lines
into it (the link tier, because its triple `peer | link_stable | proto` is already the
consolidator's fixture), an ESP32 compile with its flash/RAM delta stated, then C4.

### Phase C, increment 3: the episode tier is in the Cardputer's firmware — built, not yet flashed

*2026-10-01*

`EpisodeNode.h` (the Arduino glue, `LaneGenNode` shape) is wired into
`cardputer_console`, the only node that scores link expectations. **Every scored
PerceptLearn window is now one `@LAT103` episode**, written *beside* the existing
`@LAT92`/`@LAT91` path, which is untouched: ACT-III §C3 keeps Rule 3 as the comparison,
and the comparison now runs on the board. **No board was attached this session, so none of
this has run on hardware.**

**The mapping** (`renderLinkEpisode`, byte-pinned in `test_episode`): one sentence per
claim, `said:` with the predicted/observed medians as provenance, then
`percept: <k> | <peer> | link_stable | <proto> | <pol> | -` with met → `+`, violated → `-`,
**unobserved → `?` (held)**: counted as seen, never believed, which is what Rule 3 and
`consolidator_compare.py` already did with it, so the measured comparison carries over.
PerceptLearn gained two read-only accessors (`scoredCount()`/`scored(i)`) over the
`scored_vec_` snapshot — not `claims_`, for the reason `adoptRun()` already documents.

**Per WINDOW, not per run — a decision, stated.** ACT-III §C2's table says "one record per
closed window-run", but TTG-0003 §2 gives each *episode* one vote, so a 20-window run
written as one episode would count as 1 where Rule 3 and the comparison counted 20. A
record per window keeps the counts the comparison measured. The ring makes that
affordable: 48 retained, the rest carried.

**Two allocation choices made against the Cardputer's heap** (maxalloc ~7 KB with the
radios up, 2026-08-13):
- the checkpoint is rendered into an **exact-size `malloc`** (`checkpointBytes()`, pinned
  equal to the render length) and freed at once, not a 4.4 KB static. A failed `malloc`
  just leaves the commit due, which is harmless because the checkpoint is the commit point;
- deletes are **batched** at `SEMANTIC_CUT_SLACK` 16 dead records with a 5-minute back-off
  after a refusal, plus an unconditional cut in `setup()` **above the radios**, beside the
  scheduled-prune block and for the same reason. Measured in `test_episode` at the fleet
  constants: 20 rewrites per 400 episodes instead of 40, peak 59 records in the tier
  (against 4×48 + 24 + 16 for the lanes it replaces).

**Cost, measured against a HEAD worktree** (`esp32s3`, huge_app, 8M): **+10,892 B flash**
(1,333,805 → 1,344,697; 42% either way) and **+4,824 B static RAM** (139,644 → 144,468).
⚠ The RAM is the number to watch: ~3.4 KB is the 32-term consolidator table and 1.28 KB
the link-episode buffer, all coming out of a heap whose largest block was single-digit KB
once BLE + WiFi were up. Build clean with `--warnings all` (the one hit is a pre-existing
unused `playStartupToot`). `test_episode` 114 checks / 0 failures; suite 35/35.

**To verify on hardware** (Cardputer `50:78:7D:CE:88:10`; firmware only, so LittleFS and
its runtime store survive):

```bash
"$ACLI" compile --upload -p COMx --fqbn "esp32:esp32:esp32s3:CDCOnBoot=cdc,PartitionScheme=huge_app,FlashSize=8M" \
        --libraries firmware/libraries firmware/cardputer_console
python scratchpad/catchboot.py COMx 14
#   -> "[episode] boot in N ms: 0 live episode(s) replayed, 0 present, maxalloc ..."
```
Then, with the node **still** (Rule 1 arms only off a `still` window):
1. `[episode] live 1 present 1 …` after the first scored window, growing one per window;
2. at live 49: a fold + commit (`commits 1`), `@LAT104LON0` in a pull, `live` back to 40;
3. at 16 dead: a runtime cut — **`cuts 1` or `cut fail … last '<step>'`; either is a
   result**, and the step name says which;
4. reboot → `boot … N live episode(s) replayed` with N = the pre-reboot `live`, and the
   `[episode]` belief lines identical to before the reboot — GATE 3 on hardware;
5. `maxalloc` after the radios are up, against the 7 KB the 2026-08-13 entry recorded,
   and BLE/WiFi still working. **If the 4.8 KB costs the radios, that is the finding**,
   and the first lever is `SEMANTIC_MAX_TERMS` as a *build property* (it sizes a class, so
   a sketch `#define` would be an ODR mismatch, not a smaller table).

### Phase C, increment 3 on hardware: the first boot found the old cap starving the new tier

*2026-10-01, Cardputer `50:78:7D:CE:88:10` on COM14, firmware only (LittleFS kept)*

**Pre-flash backup** `master/cardputer_pre_episode_2026-10-01.md`: 34,812 B, clean
(0 CR, 0 NUL). ⚠ **`@LAT94` and `@LAT97` were both at 48/48 one day after the
2026-09-30 FS flash** — the treadmill C0b measured, back within a day.

**The first flash wrote nothing, and the reason was the old lane.** The link flush began
`if (laneCount(97) >= LINKPERCEPT_MAX_LANE) { gLearn.disarm(); reset; }`, so with
`@LAT97` full **no window was ever scored again** — and the episode tier, which is the
thing built never to refuse, received no evidence at all (`appended 0`). That gate was
right while `@LAT92` was the only consumer (its outcome cites an `@LAT97` record), and wrong
the moment a second consumer existed that cites nothing there. Fixed in the sketch: the
window is staged and scored regardless; only the `@LAT97` record and the `@LAT92` outcome
(whose provenance would dangle) are withheld, and the outcome is still rendered so the run
accounting stays true. 📐 General form: **a new tier wired beside an old one inherits the
old one's gates unless each is re-read for whose sake it exists.**

**Then, with peers powered** (`0x200` T-Deck, `0x100` K10, `0x010` V4-A — five
`(peer, proto)` claims): one `@LAT103` episode per scored window, `appended N (fail 0)`,
`malformed 0`, and belief lines that check by hand (`7 0 | 227` = round(255·8/9)).

✅ **GATE 3 ON HARDWARE — a reboot reproduces the beliefs byte-for-byte.** Captured the
`[episode]` belief print, reset at once, captured the boot replay: **7 episodes replayed in
82 ms, all five belief lines IDENTICAL.** (Live replay only — no checkpoint existed yet; the
checkpoint path is the next measurement.)

Observed, not yet acted on:
- **`eps:0` on every term.** EPS = sal × (255 − conf) / 255 with `sal = seen + asked`;
  at 7 episodes and conf 227, 7 × 28 / 255 floors to 0. Integer EPS is **degenerate while a
  node is young**: every term ties, so reclaim-lowest-EPS falls back to table order and an
  EPS-ranked display would show nothing. Not a fault in the counting; a resolution question
  for the arbiter/snake (§D4/§E) to settle before they read it.
- **maxalloc read 2 KB** with three peers on the air (8 KB at boot with none, 4 KB a
  minute later). No HEAD baseline *with peers* exists, so this is not yet attributable to
  the +4.8 KB; it is the number to compare when someone next runs HEAD with the fleet up.
- **Two multi-second loop passes** (`linkperc 2013 ms` right after boot, `link 3237 ms` at
  ~3 min), neither with episode work in progress (the first had zero peers and an empty
  table). Consistent with the fleet's long-standing unexplained stall, now landing inside
  named sections for the first time — recorded, not chased.

### The hour on hardware: fold, commit and boot cut work — and the Cardputer leaks ~500 B/min regardless

*2026-10-01, Cardputer, peers `0x010` V4-A, `0x100` K10, `0x200` T-Deck powered*

**✅ The checkpoint path, on hardware.** Over a 65-minute run the first fold landed exactly
where the ring says (live 49 → 41, `horizon 8`, `commits 1`), the second at `horizon 17`.
An **unplanned reset** at 3154 s then exercised the whole boot path at once: read the
`@LAT104` checkpoint, replay 43 live episodes, **cut the 9 dead records at boot**
(`cuts 1`, before the radios — the design's intended cut point). The beliefs before the
reset were `33/50/49/49/49` plus two all-met windows; after it, **`35/52/51/51/51` —
exactly the expected values. GATE 3 holds through a checkpoint, a boot cut and a crash.**
The runtime (radios-up) cut was not reached before the run ended: at 10 dead it sat
below `SEMANTIC_CUT_SLACK` 16, so **whether a rewrite succeeds at runtime on this heap is
still unmeasured.**

⚠ **Defect found: `seen` is not carried.** `eps` read 1 before that reset and 0 after:
the `carried:` line keeps for/against/episodes but not `seen`, so `sal = seen + asked`
— and with it EPS, the eviction key — drops on every reboot. The fix is a fourth number on
the `carried:` line (a format change, so it needs the parser and the gravestone tests).

🛑 **THE FINDING THE RUN WAS NOT LOOKING FOR: the Cardputer resets every ~20 minutes.**
Three `rst:0xc (RTC_SW_CPU_RST)` in the hour, each preceded by `maxalloc` decaying to
0–1 KB, and two appends refused at 129 slots of index headroom (the heap, not the index).
Free heap — never printed until today — is only **~7–11 KB** with the radios up and falls
in 250–1300 B steps a few minutes apart.

📐 **A/B on the same board, same peers, same session — it is NOT Phase C:**

| build | free heap | slope |
|---|---|---|
| `-DPHASEC_EPISODES=0` (pre-Phase-C link flush, no episode tier) | 11,284 → 6,556 B over 570 s | **≈ −500 B/min** |
| default (episode tier on) | 9,472 → 6,552 B over 361 s | **≈ −485 B/min** |

and, inside the default build, the tier's own steps measured directly: `appendLink`
**0 B** on every window, `service` (including a fold + checkpoint `malloc`/`free`) **0 B**.
A per-loop-section heap split was tried and abandoned: the radio tasks allocate on the
other core, so every section swung ±1–5 KB per 30 s around a net of −250 B — noise that
would have sent the search into `render`/`nav` for nothing.

⚠ **This contradicts the 2026-08 entry's "a persistent CEILING, not a leak — do not go
looking for a leak."** That entry read only `maxalloc`, which can sit flat while free heap
drains beneath it; today's run shows the drain. Either the leak arrived after August or
it was always there and unmeasured. Two things now make it findable: free heap is on every
`[loop]` line, and `PHASEC_EPISODES` stays in the sketch as a kill-switch for exactly this
kind of A/B.

**Consequences worth stating:**
- every Cardputer finding that assumed long uptime is suspect — including the
  `@LAT90` `STREAM-ORIGIN` churn C0b counted (a node rebooting every 20 min writes them);
- the episode tier is the one store on this board that **survives** the resets exactly
  (GATE 3 above), which is the hypothesis working, and the strongest argument for it yet;
- 📌 **Next diagnostic, needs the operator:** the same 10-minute free-heap slope with the
  **peers powered off**. The first boot today, with no peers, read `maxalloc 8K` flat; if
  the slope vanishes without peers, the leak is in the receive path (ESP-NOW/BLE per
  frame), and that narrows it to a handful of callbacks.

The board is left on the default build (episode tier on), which is no worse than
the A build for the leak and keeps the tier's evidence accumulating.

### The leak is the BLE scanner — and a periodic scan restart stops it

*2026-10-01, Cardputer, same session, four 10-minute A/B builds on one board*

| build (all with the fleet's peers OFF) | free heap over ~10 min |
|---|---|
| default — BLE advertise + continuous passive scan | ≈ **−450 B/min**, reset at ~20 min |
| `-DUSE_BLE=0` — BLE compiled out | **108,036 B, flat** |
| `-DBLELINK_SCAN=0` — advertise only | **16,516 B, flat** |
| `-DBLELINK_RESTART_MS=60000` — stop/clear/restart the scan every 60 s | **~26,400 B ±300, flat for 12 min; maxalloc 8 → 17 KB** |

**What that pins down.** With the peers off, no advert carries the fleet key, so the
sketch's BLE callback never ran: the drain came from the scanner handling **foreign**
adverts (phones and the like) on its own. The Arduino wrapper's per-result path reads
clean — with `wantDuplicates=true` each `BLEAdvertisedDevice` is `new`ed and `delete`d, and
its copy/assign/destructor all `free` the payload — so the accumulation is below it, in the
Bluedroid scan path, and a restart makes the stack release it. That is a mitigation by
measurement, not a root cause; the line that leaks is still unnamed.

**Fixed (Cardputer):** `BLELINK_RESTART_MS` defaults to 60 000 in `BleLink.cpp`, and the
Cardputer's `loop()` now calls `blelink::loop()`. ⚠ It takes effect **only on a board that
calls `blelink::loop()`** — the V4s, T-Deck and K10 do not, so their firmware is unchanged
(all three compile-checked). `USE_BLE` became `#ifndef`-overridable for the A/B, and
`BLELINK_SCAN` is a library switch; both default on.

📐 **Two things this changes beyond the Cardputer:**
- **BLE costs this board ~92 KB of heap just by being on** (108 KB without it vs 16.5 KB
  advertise-only). That is the "unexplained 7–8 KB maxalloc ceiling" of 2026-08 — not a
  mystery, a radio stack — and it is the budget every Cardputer feature has been fighting.
- **V4-A's 2026-08 decline (`maxalloc` 101 KB → 38 KB over 2 h 29 m) has the same shape.**
  The V4s run the same `BleLink`; with ~100 KB of headroom they would take hours, not
  minutes, to reach zero. Unverified — the next board to measure with free heap printed,
  and the next to give `blelink::loop()` to if it drains.

📌 **Still to verify with the peers ON:** that fleet adverts are still heard across
restarts (the `proto:ble` claims keep appearing in episodes) and that the heap stays flat
under real fleet traffic, where the callback does run.

*2026-10-01, V4-A `8C:FD:49:B7:AC:F4` on COM6, 15 min per build*

| build | free heap |
|---|---|

| default (`blelink::loop()` every pass, 60 s restart) | **exactly 145,172 B after every restart, for 15 min** (≈137 KB mid-cycle) |
