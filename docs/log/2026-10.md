### Phase C, increment 2: the EPISODE lane, and why the checkpoint is the commit point

*2026-10-01*

ACT-III §C2's second tier is built: `firmware/libraries/Semantic/src/Episode.{h,cpp}`,
portable and native-tested, plus one new `Ttdb` primitive. **Not yet wired into a sketch
and not yet compiled for the ESP32** — it is a library the next increment calls.

**The lanes.** `@LAT103` EPISODE (one `ttdb-episode` block per record, TTG-RFC-0002 §5.1)
and `@LAT104` CARRIED (the fold checkpoint). Below 90 is a node record to both globes;
`@LAT102` is reserved for attributed testimony. They sit beside `@LAT93`–`97` during the
transition, and C0's deletion removes those, not these.

**The design problem the increment was really about.** C2c's fold-before-forget was built
in increment 1 as a move in RAM (live → carried, total invariant). On flash it meets the
fact that a `Ttdb` has exactly one way to delete: a whole-file rewrite with seven named
failure modes, three of which are "out of heap with the radios up", measured on hardware.
A fold that is only safe if the delete succeeds would reproduce the deferred-prune
machinery C5 is meant to delete. So the order is:

1. **FOLD** in RAM (replay the oldest live episodes `EVICTING`).
2. **COMMIT** by *appending* one `@LAT104` checkpoint: `through: <ordinal>` + every term's
   carried tally. An append is the one write a `Ttdb` does not rewrite for.
3. **CUT** — `Ttdb::removeCuts` — episodes at or below the **committed** horizon, and every
   checkpoint but the newest.

Boot reads only the newest checkpoint and **skips** episodes at or below its `through:`
even if they are still on flash. A cut therefore stops being bookkeeping and becomes
garbage collection: a crash between any two steps, or a cut the heap refuses forever, costs
space and nothing else. That is the property the deferred-prune-on-next-boot NVS machinery
was approximating, obtained by ordering instead of by retry.

**Three decisions made in passing, each written into `Episode.h`:**

- **Oldest-first eviction, not lowest-EPS.** C2c says eviction must not be recency. For
  *episodes* the order cannot move a belief (TTG-0003 §2 is a sum and the fold is a move);
  it only chooses which provenance survives. Oldest-first keeps the retained window
  contiguous, so the horizon is **one integer** rather than a set on flash, and it never
  re-points a citation — ordinals are max+1, the oldest is never the max, so unlike a
  `@LAT94`–`97` prune (lane empties, restarts at LON0) no `@LAT100` boundary is needed.
  Lowest-EPS stays where it belongs: the TERM tier's reclaim.
- **Ordinals are serial numbers mod 32768.** `TtdbRecord::lon` is `int16_t`, and a lane
  that never restarts reaches 32767 in ~23 days at one episode a minute. Every older/newer
  is an age measured back from the newest record in **file order**. ⚠ This makes a raw
  ordinal citation ambiguous across a wrap, so **C4's `follows@` edges should carry a sid**
  (TTDB-RFC-0010 §4.3), not a bare ordinal.
- **Only the newest checkpoint is ever read.** A term the TERM tier reclaimed is absent
  from later checkpoints; reading an older one too would resurrect a belief the store
  chose to forget. `CheckpointReader` honours exactly one ordinal.

**The gravestone rule holds in three independent layers** (no checkpoint line begins
`percept:`/`belief:`; a `carried:` line has four pipe columns where a percept needs six;
and the reader feeds only `ttdb-episode` blocks on `@LAT103`). Each is tested on its own.

**Measured, `tests/test_episode.cpp` — 82 checks, 0 failures.** The protocol runs end to
end against a stand-in store, with a **reboot from the store alone after every episode**:

| scenario | episodes | commits (failed) | cuts (failed) | max on flash |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean, ring 8 / batch 3 | 300 | 73 (0) | 73 (0) | 8 |
| cuts refused half the time | 300 | 73 (0) | 145 (72) | 9 |
| commits fail 1 in 3 | 300 | 109 (36) | 73 (0) | 9 |
| both | 300 | 109 (36) | 145 (72) | 10 |
| across the 32768 wrap, both faults | 200 | 71 (23) | 95 (47) | 10 |
| fleet constants 48 / 8, cuts refused half | 400 | 40 (0) | 78 (39) | 49 |

In every scenario: every episode appended (none refused), the node's beliefs equal a
never-evicting reference at every step, and the reboot agrees at every step. Worst-case
checkpoint (32 terms, maximal lemmas and tallies) is **3,945 B of a 4,416 B buffer**,
pinned both ways (fits; one byte short writes nothing).

📐 **Negative controls, both red:** cutting at the RAM horizon instead of the committed
one is caught by the reboot gate (only in the commit-failure scenarios, which is exactly
where it is wrong); a boot that ignores the horizon fails 17 checks. `test_ttdb_index`
gains a `removeCuts` section (10 checks, through the real `Ttdb` via `tests/shim/`),
including that file order survives the rewrite, since the ring reads the newest episode
off it. Suite **35/35**.

📎 Housekeeping: `tests/Makefile`'s `clean` recipe had carried literal `\n` text instead of

**What is next, in order:** the sketch glue (`EpisodeNode.h`, the `LaneGenNode` shape —

### The leak is the BLE scanner — and a periodic scan restart stops it

*2026-10-01, Cardputer, same session, four 10-minute A/B builds on one board*

| build (all with the fleet's peers OFF) | free heap over ~10 min |
|---|---|
| default — BLE advertise + continuous passive scan | ≈ **−450 B/min**, reset at ~20 min |
| `-DUSE_BLE=0` — BLE compiled out | **108,036 B, flat** |
| `-DBLELINK_SCAN=0` — advertise only | **16,516 B, flat** |
| `-DBLELINK_RESTART_MS=60000` — stop/clear/restart the scan every 60 s | **~26,400 B ±300, flat for 12 min; maxalloc 8 → 17 KB** |

**What that pins down.** With the peers off, no advert carries the fleet key, so the
sketch's BLE callback never ran: the drain came from the scanner handling **foreign**
adverts (phones and the like) on its own. The Arduino wrapper's per-result path reads
clean — with `wantDuplicates=true` each `BLEAdvertisedDevice` is `new`ed and `delete`d, and
its copy/assign/destructor all `free` the payload — so the accumulation is below it, in the
Bluedroid scan path, and a restart makes the stack release it. That is a mitigation by
measurement, not a root cause; the line that leaks is still unnamed.

**Fixed (Cardputer):** `BLELINK_RESTART_MS` defaults to 60 000 in `BleLink.cpp`, and the
Cardputer's `loop()` now calls `blelink::loop()`. ⚠ It takes effect **only on a board that
calls `blelink::loop()`** — the V4s, T-Deck and K10 do not, so their firmware is unchanged
(all three compile-checked). `USE_BLE` became `#ifndef`-overridable for the A/B, and
`BLELINK_SCAN` is a library switch; both default on.

📐 **Two things this changes beyond the Cardputer:**
- **BLE costs this board ~92 KB of heap just by being on** (108 KB without it vs 16.5 KB
  advertise-only). That is the "unexplained 7–8 KB maxalloc ceiling" of 2026-08 — not a
  mystery, a radio stack — and it is the budget every Cardputer feature has been fighting.
- **V4-A's 2026-08 decline (`maxalloc` 101 KB → 38 KB over 2 h 29 m) has the same shape.**
  The V4s run the same `BleLink`; with ~100 KB of headroom they would take hours, not
  minutes, to reach zero. Unverified — the next board to measure with free heap printed,
  and the next to give `blelink::loop()` to if it drains.

📌 **Still to verify with the peers ON:** that fleet adverts are still heard across
restarts (the `proto:ble` claims keep appearing in episodes) and that the heap stays flat
under real fleet traffic, where the callback does run.

### Two Phase C fixes: `seen` survives a fold and a reboot, and a stamp names its frame

*2026-10-01, native-tested, then flashed to the Cardputer the same day — hardware result at the end*

**1. The `seen`/EPS drift was three defects, not one.** The hardware symptom was `eps 1 → 0`
across a reboot; reading the code found:

- `percept()` counted `seen` on **every** call, so a fold's `EVICTING` replay counted the
  evicted episode's percepts **again** — `seen` was inflated at runtime by every fold;
- the checkpoint did not carry `seen`, so a reboot **dropped** the evicted share;
- `episodes` had the mirror defect: the `carried:` line wrote the term's TOTAL, and a reboot
  then added the live replay on top — **double-counted**.

Fixed the way for/against already were: a per-episode scratch (`ep_seen_`) and a
`carried_seen`/`carried_episodes` split, so `EVICTING` *moves* the episode's share and the
total is invariant. The `carried:` line is now `<for> <against> <episodes> <seen>` — the
carried shares only — and **a pre-fix 3-number line still parses** (seen 0), because one is
on the Cardputer's flash right now. ⚠ That old line's third number was a total, so
`episodes` over-reports once on the first boot after the flash, until the next checkpoint
is written from RAM; `episodes` feeds no conf or EPS.

📐 **The gate was blind to it, so the gate changed first.** `test_episode`'s comparison
now includes `seen`, `episodes` and `eps` per term, not just the belief line: on the old
code it went **red with 18 failures** (GATE 2 — the fold inflation; GATE 3 — the reboot
drop), and green after the fix. **116 checks, 0 failures.** The hardware gate passed for the
same reason the native one did: both compared only what the belief line prints.
Cost: +312 B flash, +200 B RAM.

**2. C4's open question, answered: the frame is `downbeat_epoch`, not (`conductor`, `era`).**
Pulse.cpp settles it: `era` bumps on a scene change and on a takeover, neither of which moves
the clock; `downbeat_epoch` is minted exactly once, by a **fresh** self-appointment — the
only place a new frame is born — and carried unchanged through every beacon, handoff and
scene change. So a stamp is now `at: <t> ±<b> frame:<downbeat>`; stamps order by their
ranges only when both name the same frame, a bar admits only episodes in its own frame, and
a frameless bounded stamp (those written before this) orders nothing across agents. A lone
node that self-appointed and later joined the fleet's lineage therefore **cannot** mis-order
its old stamps — they sit in another frame. A `follows` edge still orders across frames
(knowledge is not a clock). ⚠ Residual, stated in FleetTime.h: two independently-founded
lineages can mint the same downbeat (same beat of their own clocks); a founder id on the
PULSE wire would close it. `test_fleettime` 34 checks; item 4's agreement still holds.

✅ **On hardware (Cardputer, same day).** Boot read the pre-fix 3-number checkpoint
(`through: 134`) with `malformed 0`. New `@LAT103` episodes carry `at: 52895 ±23 frame:5000`
as a follower and `at: 172892 ±0 frame:5000` after the Cardputer took the baton — **the
frame held across a takeover**, which is the reason it is `downbeat_epoch` and not `era`.
The first fold then committed `through: 143` in the new format:

### Delivery scaling: a WANT queue at the author, a hard cap per author (2026-10-04)

The operator approved options 1 and 2 (option 3, a longer settle, only if the next six-board
run still falls short).
1. **`WantQueue` (5 slots, 4 usable)** replaces the author's single WANT slot. One slot
   dropped every WANT that arrived while another was pending. With four or five boards
   asking one author, each dropped asker waited out a 4 s timeout: most of V4-B's 574
   `unanswered`. Same single-producer pattern as `VectorInbox` (the callback publishes
   `head_` last). `pop()` **skips a WANT superseded by a newer one from the same asker**:
   that asker has moved on, and the old slices would be ignored anyway. The `[deliver]` line
   gains `wantq drop … superseded …`.
2. **The hard cap scales with authors:** `heldHardCap(authors, board_max)` =
   16 × authors held, never under 56, never over the board's ceiling (`setHeldHardMax`,
   ≤ `HELD_HARD_MAX` 80). The **Cardputer is set to 64**: its index runs 221/288, and 64
   leaves ~30 free. The other five use 80. The line shows `cap N`.
- Native **122 checks**. New ones cover both askers served in order, a full queue dropping
  and counting, an older WANT superseded, the cap formula and its clamps, and `authors()`.
  The case from the hardware run: 60 unrecorded copies of four authors are cut early under
  the fixed 56, and **not at all** under the scaled 64. **Suspected mechanism for run 8 (code reading, not yet tested).** The S3 builds use HWCDC
(`usb_mode=1`). In core 3.3.10 `HWCDC::write()` drops without blocking when no USB host is
present. But when a host is attached and **nobody reads the port**, it retries
`tx_timeout_ms` (100 ms) up to 20 times: **a 2 s block per write** once the TX ring is full.
No sketch calls `Serial.setTxTimeoutMs`. The bridge calls `gSerial.writeFrame` **inside the
ESP-NOW receive callback** (v4a_bridge.ino:618), so a blocked write stalls reception, and every
`Serial.printf` in `loop()` stalls serving and fetching. Writing its own episodes needs no
radio, which matches run 8: V4-A deaf and mute while it kept authoring. Whether V4-A was on
the laptop's USB last night isn't known. 📎 The unexplained ~2 s loop stall (`worst pass 2009ms`,
CLAUDE.md) equals this 20 × 100 ms. That's a hypothesis to test, not a finding.

**Test running:** V4-A left on the laptop's USB with the port closed from 09:42:20 (uptime
~11 600 s, baseline `fetched 885`, `served 2828 of 2852`). The port is reopened passively 45
min later. The cumulative counters then show what it did while unread.

**Result (10:27): NOT reproduced.** After 2751 s (45.9 min) unread on the laptop's USB, the
cumulative counters show V4-A kept working at its watched rates:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| fetched | 885 | 1068 | 4.0/min | ~4/min |
| served / WANTs | 2828 / 2852 | 3441 / 3481 | 13.4/min | 13.1/min |
| own seq | 730 | 779 | 1.1/min | ~1/min |
| bars written | 19 | 23 | — | — |

So a host that doesn't read the port does **not** silence V4-A. That doesn't explain run 8.
